# 6×6 Medium Format, color Film Stock: developed-look spec (handoff for the coding agent)

**Format Reference (internal only, ADR 0015):** Hasselblad 500C with its 80mm lens, loaded with Kodak Portra 400.
Never show the camera or film name in the product. All shipped files use `6x6_color` naming.
**Date:** 2026-10-12. **Status:** proposed values for DEC-04. Not yet compared with real 6×6 Portra 400 scans.

## TL;DR

- **Ship one 33³ LUT through the same path as the 16mm Cinema.**
  - The file is `immerse_6x6_color_scan_applelog_33.cube`. Its input is the Apple Log curve on Rec.2020 primaries, and its output is sRGB.
  - It is generated by `portra400_lut.py` (numpy only, with a `--test` self-test).
  - It uses the **same `film()` model** as the 250D generator, so the app needs one LUT sampler and one pre-pass for every Camera.
- **The look is a modern lab scan of the negative, not an optical print.** That means:
  - gentle contrast and slightly lifted, very slightly cool blacks;
  - highlights that keep detail 5 stops over grey (Portra's famous overexposure latitude);
  - cream-warm greys;
  - quiet reds, pastel cyan-leaning blues, muted purples and magentas, softer greens.

  The print-on-Endura look is kept in code as a QA reference only. It is far more contrasty than the PRD's "gentle contrast."
- **How the numbers were made:**
  1. I fitted the model to a datasheet-driven spectral simulation of Portra 400 ([spectral_film_lut](https://github.com/JanLohse/spectral_film_lut), MIT). Fit error is ΔE2000 1.4 on greys and 2.8 on a ColorChecker.
  2. I then applied a hand-tuned "lab operator" finishing layer: +¼ stop, ~+300K warmth, grey-balanced, calmer reds and greens.

  The finished look sits about **ΔE 6.6** from the raw simulation, mostly from the brightness and warmth. Every finishing value is labeled **[T]** and should be checked against real scans.
- **Effects for 6×6:**
  - grain only, very fine: ≈0.4× the 16mm per-pixel amplitude at 12 MP;
  - mild lens vignette;
  - square, borderless 1:1;
  - **no** halation, weave, flicker, dust or aging (PRD §6.1/6.3, ADR 0013).

**Source labels:**
- **[P]** published by Kodak, Apple or ITU.
- **[P-d]** derived from published curves as digitized in spectral_film_lut.
- **[F]** fitted to the spectral simulation.
- **[S]** secondary source (lab, magazine, reviewer).
- **[T]** tuned by me; validate.

## 1. What the Format Reference film is

**From Kodak's E-4050 datasheet** ([January 2025 revision](https://www.freestylephoto.com/static/pdf/product_pdf/kodak/e4050_Kodak_Portra_400.pdf)):

- **Speed and balance:** ISO 400, daylight-balanced, process C-41. It needs no filter or exposure correction from 1/10,000 s to 1 s. [P]
- **Positioning:** Kodak calls it the world's finest-grain 400-speed color negative, built on its VISION motion-picture film technology and designed for scanning. [P]
- **Print Grain Index:** 25 is the visual threshold for graininess, and 4 units is one just-noticeable difference. [P]

  | Format | 4×6 print | 8×10 print | 16×20 print |
  |---|---|---|---|
  | 135 | 37 | 59 | 89 |
  | 120 6×6 | **25** (2.6× magnification) | 37 | 59 |

  At normal print sizes, grain on 6×6 sits right at the threshold of visibility. That is the basis for "very fine grain" below.
- **Intended print path:** the film is optimized for printing to Kodak Endura Premier paper. A grey card should read 0.77–0.87 red density (Status M). [P]
- **Characteristic curve:**
  - The straight-line slopes are R 0.556, G 0.550, B 0.641. [P-d]
  - The toe starts about 4 stops under grey, and the straight line continues past 6 stops over grey. [P-d]
  - The steeper blue record is why uncorrected greys drift warm in shadows and cool in highlights. Labs remove that drift, and so does this LUT.

**From reviewers and labs:**

- **Latitude:** Portra 400's latitude is lopsided toward overexposure. [S]
  - [Shutterbug](https://www.shutterbug.com/node/420619) found 2 stops over barely noticeable and 4 over still very usable, while 2 under was already struggling.
  - [Fstoppers](https://fstoppers.com/film/testing-limits-kodaks-portra-400-438727) reports usable results even at 6 over.
  - Hence the long, unclipped highlight shoulder.
- **Scanner character:** [S]
  - **Frontier SP-3000:** golden skin, cooler cyan or blue shadows, bluer greens, deeper blacks and punchier color ([Carmencita](https://carmencitafilmlab.com/?p=35292), [Optik Oldschool](https://www.optik-oldschool.com/blogs/blog/why-we-prefer-frontier-scans-for-opticolour200)).
  - **Noritsu HS-1800:** flatter, warmer overall, peach or pink skin, softer color and more accurate greens.
  - This spec sits between the two: warm overall like Noritsu, with gentle Frontier-style cool shadows and bluer skies. **Open decision:** pick one scanner as part of the Format Reference so "does it match?" has a single answer.

## 2. Pipeline in the app

This is identical to the 16mm Cinema spec except for the effect passes.

```
photo in
  HEIC/JPEG: Display P3 (sRGB transfer) -> linear -> inverse_knee() -> P3->Rec.2020 matrix
  ProRAW/DNG (scene-linear): -> camera->Rec.2020 matrix, NO inverse knee
-> lens vignette (linear)                  §5
-> optional softening, sigma 0.25 px        §5
-> Apple Log encode
-> grain (in Apple Log units)               §5
-> 3D LUT, tetrahedral
-> crop 1:1, borderless
-> encode: 10-bit HEIC preferred; if 8-bit, keep the grain (it dithers) and never denoise after this
```

- **`inverse_knee`:** a copy is in `portra400_lut.py`. It is identity below 0.6. Above that it is `0.6 + d/(1 − d/0.5)` with `d = y − 0.6`, clamped to `d ≤ 0.45`, so phone white maps to scene 2.6 (about 3.85 stops over grey). [T]
- **P3→Rec.2020 matrix:** compute it with colour-science. Approximately `[[0.7539,0.1986,0.0476],[0.0457,0.9418,0.0125],[-0.0012,0.0176,0.9836]]`.
- **ProRAW gives the most authentic result.** The LUT's highlight shoulder then works on real scene data instead of re-expanded phone highlights.

## 3. LUT constants (default "scan" look)

This is the same `film()` function as the 250D generator, with three changes:
- a per-channel negative gamma;
- a "scan" output curve option, `D = D_GREY·exp(−K·d)`, alongside the logistic print curve;
- a display black-level lift.

| Constant | Value | Label | Meaning |
|---|---|---|---|
| `NEG_GAMMA` | R 0.556, G 0.550, B 0.641 | [P-d] | Negative straight-line slopes |
| `EXPOSURE` | +0.25 stop | [T] | Scanned slightly bright, the way Portra is usually shot and scanned |
| `WB_GAIN` | 1.025, 1.000, 0.955 | [T] | About +300K warmth |
| `TOE_STOPS` | −1.08 | [F] | Soft negative toe |
| `XTALK` (log space) | `[[1.1919,−0.25,0.0581],[0.1446,0.6054,0.25],[0.0511,−0.0468,0.9957]]` | [F] | Interlayer crosstalk. Rows sum to 1, so greys stay grey. The green row's off-diagonals sit at the fit bound (±0.25). |
| `K` | 3.90, 3.98, 3.31 | [F→T] | Fit was 3.86/3.98/3.98. Blue is rebalanced by 0.550/0.641 to stop grey crossover, then trimmed 3% for warm highlights and cool shadows. |
| `D_GREY` | 0.80 | [F] | Grey output density (fit 0.823) |
| `BIAS` | 0, 0, 0 | [T] | Fit was −0.010 / 0 / +0.004; removed (lab grey balance) |
| `BLACK` | 0.0015, 0.0015, 0.0017 (display-linear) | [T] | Lifted, very slightly cool scan black |
| `BANDS` (fit) + `FINISH_BANDS` [T] | see `portra400_lut.py` | [F]+[T] | OKLCh hue, chroma and lightness gains per hue band |
| `HI_DESAT` / `SH_DESAT` | 0.25 / 0.15 | [T] | Creamy highlights, quieter shadows |
| Highlight soft-clip | knee 0.85, tanh | [T] | |

**What the color stage does to mid-saturation colors**

Measured through the shipped LUT in OKLab, relative to a neutral of the same luma:

| Color | Hue shift | Chroma | Lightness |
|---|---|---|---|
| Red | +2.7° (toward orange) | ×1.02 | +0.03 |
| Orange / skin | −2.4° | ×0.95 | ≈0 |
| Yellow | −7.6° (toward orange) | ×0.73 | −0.04 |
| Green | +1.1° | ×0.82 | −0.10 |
| Aqua | ≈0 | ×1.01 | −0.06 |
| **Blue** | **−21.6° (toward cyan)** | **×0.69** | **+0.06** |
| Purple | +4.8° | ×0.57 | +0.11 |
| Magenta | +1.5° | ×0.67 | +0.08 |

**Sanity values** (sRGB out for a neutral scene-linear grey) are encoded in `SANITY` and asserted by `--test`. The tolerance is ±0.02.

| Stops vs 18% grey | −6 | −2 | 0 | +2 | +3.85 (phone white) | +5 |
|---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
| R, G, B out | .080/.078/.086 | .210/.204/.206 | **.488/.480/.470** | .792/.789/.776 | .932/.931/.925 | .965/.965/.962 |

The self-test also checks that the neutral ramp is monotonic from −8 to +7 stops and that the .cube file has n³ rows.

**Print reference look** (`--look print`, Endura Premier paper; fit only):
- Grey is 0.507 and highlights reach white by about +3 stops. The blacks are deep and saturation is high.
- Its fit error is ΔE 0.87 on greys and 2.8 on the chart.
- Use it to sanity-check hue relationships, not as the product look.

## 4. Verification done

- **Fit vs. simulation, scan path** (ΔE2000):
  - greys: mean 1.40, max 2.14;
  - ColorChecker at −3…+3 stops: mean 2.81, p95 6.30;
  - 2,500 random surface colors: mean 3.51, p95 7.65.
- **Finished look vs. simulation, ColorChecker:** mean ΔE 6.6, max 10.2 (blue flower). Light skin is 8.3, mostly from the +¼ stop and the warmth. This is deliberate, but it is the biggest open taste call.
- **LUT path agreement:** the Apple Log 33³ path and the sRGB-input preview 33³ path differ by mean ΔE 0.3–0.4 (p99 under 1) on test photos.
- **LUT size:** 33³ vs. 65³ differs by mean ΔE 0.1–0.3. **33³ is enough for photos.**
- **Visual check:** `immerse_6x6_color_preview.png` shows the phone original, the scan look and the print reference on a ColorChecker with an exposure ramp, plus three photos, square-cropped.

## 5. 6×6 effect passes

All are [T] unless labeled. `W` is the output frame width in pixels. The default assumes a 3024 × 3024 square crop from a 12 MP sensor.

**Grain** (Apple Log domain, before the LUT; same shader as the 16mm spec)
- **Amplitude:** per-pixel σ in Apple Log units is **R 0.0011, G 0.0014, B 0.0025**.
  - It is derived from the 16mm values (0.0028/0.0034/0.0062 at 1920 px on a 12.52 mm frame), scaled by film area per pixel (6.5 µm vs. 18.5 µm pitch = ×0.35) and by ×1.15 for 400 vs. 250 speed. [D/T]
  - The blue ≈ 2× green ratio is kept from Kodak's per-layer granularity.
- **Size:** blur white noise with σ = **0.00027·W** (≈0.8 px at 3024), then **renormalize to unit variance**. Add a second octave at 2.5× the size with 25% weight. Bake 8 tiles and use a new random tile and offset per photo.
- **Visibility:**
  - At strength 1.0 the grain is ~0.7 8-bit code values RMS at grey. That matches Kodak's "threshold" PGI 25 for 6×6, and is barely visible at 100% zoom.
  - **Product default: `grainStrength = 1.5`** [T], so it survives HEIC compression and reads as "very fine."
  - Never exceed 0.6× the 16mm Cinema's grain.

**Lens vignette** (Planar 80 mm-style; linear light, before the LUT)
- `rgb *= 1 − 0.18·pow(smoothstep(0.30, 1.0, r), 1.6)`, where r = 1 at the corners. That is about −0.3 stop in the corners. [T]
- Keep it subtle; the square frame makes corners prominent.

**Sharpness**
- Medium format is sharp, so add no blur by default. Optionally use a Gaussian σ 0.25 px to take the edge off phone over-sharpening.
- Disable the phone's own sharpening and noise reduction where the capture API allows.
- No synthesized background blur (PRD: focus is optical only).

**Explicitly off** for this Camera: halation (still film has an anti-halation layer, and the PRD lists none), gate weave, flicker, dust and hair, fading or yellowing (ADR 0013).

**Frame:** 1:1, borderless (PRD 6.1). The 6×6 nominal frame is about 56 × 56 mm. Crop from the center of the 4:3 capture after the LUT.

## 7. Existing presets and LUTs

- **spectral_film_lut** (MIT, `pip install spectral_film_lut`):
  - Has digitized Portra 400, Portra Endura and Endura Premier data. This is what the fit used, and it can be shipped with the MIT notice.
  - Gotcha in v0.18.12: the "custom inversion" option crashes headless (it falls back to an unknown upsampling method). Use the default ACES-style inversion and pass `upsampling_method="SFL upsampling"`.
  - The author notes that interlayer and masking behavior is simplified because Kodak doesn't publish it. That is why the crosstalk fit hits its bounds.
- **RawTherapee Film Simulation Collection** (Pat David HaldCLUTs; includes Portra 160/400/800):
  - Contributors on pixls.us describe the Portra 400 clut as [a blend of several efforts tuned to look pleasing](https://discuss.pixls.us/t/the-source-of-film-simulations/13583), not a measured match.
  - Its license was not checked. Don't use it as ground truth.
- **Commercial packs** (VSCO-style presets, paid Portra LUTs): end-user licenses. Don't ship them.
