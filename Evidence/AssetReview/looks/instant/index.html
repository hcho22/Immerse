1970s Instant

Each scene developed by the app's own renderer (treatment version film-look-3) beside the phone capture it started from and photographs made with the Camera's Format Reference.

Approved by the captain on 2026-10-10 ("faded", then "approved"): the faded look, his exception to ADR 0013 and to the PRD wording below (ADR 0016). Its contrast and the size of its print-to-print variation stay provisional until the study of preserved 1970s prints.

Internal review for DEC-04 and QA-16. The Format Reference names and every reference image stay internal and never ship in the app. Reference images are public-domain or openly licensed photographs credited under each one; many are scans of surviving prints or reels, which have aged, so judge them for the format's character as it was new. The test scenes are modern digital photographs, not captures from an iPhone running the app, so capture behavior (fixed exposure, the real flash) is not shown.

Format Reference
The original 1970s Polaroid integral print (SX-70 film), as it looked fresh from the camera
PRD 2.1 section 6
Square picture on a white card that is always part of the print. Brilliant, warm, saturated color and soft detail, with a slight variation in color and density from print to print. No uneven-chemistry or spread flaws. The contrast and the size of the variation wait on a study of preserved 1970s prints.
What to judge
  • Color: brilliant, warm and saturated, the print as it was fresh, not as surviving prints have faded.
  • Detail: soft, without grain.
  • Variation: each print slightly different in color and density; never a flaw.
  • Contrast and variation stay provisional until the study of preserved 1970s prints (DEC-04).

Values in the app

From FilmLook.swift: the one place each Camera's and Film Stock's look is set. Spatial values are fractions of the picture's long edge.

    /// 1970s Instant. Format Reference: the original 1970s integral print. By the captain's choice on its review board
    /// on 2026-10-10 ("faded"), it develops as surviving prints look, not as fresh ones did (ADR 0016, an exception to
    /// ADR 0013 and to PRD 2.1 section 6.1's "brilliant, warm, saturated color"): lifted, milky blacks, softened
    /// whites, low contrast, muted color with aqua blues and olive greens, blue in the shadows and yellow in the
    /// highlights, soft detail and a coarse texture. Each print still differs slightly in color and density, with no
    /// uneven-chemistry or spread flaws. This is its current look and current contrast: its contrast and the size of
    /// that variation stay provisional until a study of preserved 1970s prints (DEC-04).
    static let instant = Treatment(
        emulsion: .color(ColorEmulsion(
            warmth: 0.09, tint: -0.005, layers: Layers(redFromGreen: 0.12, greenFromBlue: 0.15, blueFromGreen: 0.15),
            saturation: 0.85,
            tone: ToneCurve(contrast: 0.82, midtones: 0.88, black: 0.13, white: 0.9),
            shadows: RGB(0, 0.008, 0.03), highlights: RGB(0.012, 0.004, -0.03),
            grain: Grain(amplitude: 4.5, size: 0.0014, chroma: 0.3)
        )),
        lens: Lens(softness: 0.0011, edgeSoftness: 0.001, edgeDarkening: 0.4),
        printVariation: PrintVariation(density: 0.12, warmth: 0.025, tint: 0.01)
    )
