# Look review sources

Openly licensed reference imagery and test scenes for the side-by-side look review boards.
Every file is listed in `sources.json` beside this file (the working `manifest.json`) with its file page, download URL, author, licence, date and original size, and since 2026-10-13 with the SHA-256 and byte count of the file as downloaded.
References (`references/`) are real photographs or film made with each original format; scenes (`scenes/`) are modern digital captures to run through the renderer.

## Licences

Only public domain, CC0, CC BY and CC BY-SA files are used; no Unsplash or Pexels files were needed.
Licences were read from the Wikimedia Commons API (extmetadata) or from Flickr's oEmbed endpoint at download time.
CC BY and CC BY-SA files need attribution (author, title, licence, link) if anything derived from them is shown outside internal review, and CC BY-SA derivatives must keep the same licence.
Several files show identifiable people; they are fine for internal look review but would need a separate check before any external use.

## Gaps and substitutions

- Disposable: no 1990s FunSaver 800 or Kodak Max 800 scans; 2009 FunSaver frames (film unstated) plus a 2019 Kodak-branded single-use camera with 800 film are used.
- Instant: no fresh-looking original 1970s SX-70 print scanned with its frame; framed examples are expired Time-Zero film, and the 1970s examples are cropped to the picture.
- Super 8: Kodachrome II is stated for one frame grab only; the Phu Cat reel is inferred Kodachrome II; no Kodak Instamatic movie camera is confirmed; no usable gate dust or hair example.
- 16 mm colour: no 16 mm Vision3 250D; 16 mm Vision3 200T/500T footage (second tier) and 35 mm Vision3 250D stills (third tier) instead.
- 16 mm black and white: no 16 mm Double-X; 16 mm Tri-X reversal footage (alternative stock) and 35 mm Double-X stills (third tier) instead.
- Prelinger Archives home movies were not used because they carry no open licence on archive.org.

## Disposable (Kodak FunSaver / Kodak single-use, 800-speed colour negative)

Four images are genuine Kodak FunSaver frames (Flickr user danisabella, 2009) covering daylight fill flash, indoor night flash and plain daylight.
The photographer does not state the film; FunSavers sold in the US in 2009 came loaded with Kodak 800-speed colour negative film, so 800 speed is likely but not confirmed.
Two images come from a generic Kodak single-use camera sold as the 'Kodak Flash Single Use Camera' with Kodak 800 film (Matthew Paul Argall, 2019), used because openly licensed FunSaver indoor-flash frames are scarce.
All six look freshly processed (lab scans, no fading).
No openly licensed scans of 1990s FunSaver 800 or Kodak Max 800 prints turned up on Commons, Flickr (via Openverse) or the Smithsonian.

## Instant (Polaroid SX-70 integral prints)

Original 1970s SX-70 film: instant-01 (1976) and instant-02 (1978) are stated SX-70 photos, and instant-03 and instant-04 (both 1978) are square Polaroid prints whose SX-70 film type is inferred from format and date.
These four are scans of the image area only; the white frame is cropped off, and the two Alan Light scans are small (about 600 px).
Preservation: instant-03 is well preserved, instant-01 and instant-02 are mildly aged (muted, slightly warm), and instant-04 is strongly yellowed.
For the whole card with its white frame, instant-05 and instant-06 are full-print scans, but they are on long-expired Time-Zero film (the SX-70-camera film made 1980-2006), so use them for card geometry rather than colour.
No openly licensed scan of a fresh-looking 1970s SX-70 or Time-Zero print including its frame was found; the SX-70 works checked at The Met were not public domain, and the Smithsonian's open-access Polaroids have no images online.

## Super 8 (Kodachrome era)

Super 8 Kodachrome footage under open licences is scarce.
Prelinger Archives home movies on archive.org were checked but rejected: the recent home-movie uploads carry no licence, and Prelinger's own rights page says items without a Creative Commons dedication are not free to reuse.
super8-frame-01 is the only source that explicitly states Super 8 Kodachrome II (1972 frame grab); it has a warm red-brown cast from age or the transfer.
The Phu Cat Air Base reel (1966-67) is Super 8 from the period when Kodachrome II was Kodak's only colour Super 8 cartridge stock, so Kodachrome II is likely but inferred; it is a soft projection-style transfer with some yellowing and clipped highlights.
The 1977 USA travel film is Super 8 with an unstated stock (possibly Kodachrome 40 or Ektachrome) and is a low-resolution SD transfer.
No source confirms a Kodak Instamatic movie camera.
Gate dust and hair examples were not found in usable quality; an archive.org CC0 'found and damaged Super 8' item (super8footage) was checked but its transfers were too dark and soft to show dust clearly, so it was not included.

## How this was gathered

Sources were searched on Wikimedia Commons (API and categories), Flickr via Openverse, archive.org (advanced search with licence filter), the Smithsonian Open Access API and The Met collection API.
Commons stills wider than 1920 px were downloaded as the 1920 px Commons thumbnail (Commons now serves fixed thumbnail widths, so 1920 replaces the requested 2000); smaller Commons files were downloaded as originals.
Flickr files were downloaded as the original when it is at most about 2100 px, otherwise as Flickr's 2048 px rendition.
Every image was checked with `sips` and every video with `ffprobe`; files that failed to decode were deleted.
Video stills were extracted with ffmpeg at the timestamps recorded in each manifest entry (`source_timestamp`).

## Added on 2026-10-13: the Instant print study

Thirty-four scans of preserved 1970s integral instant prints, gathered for the study of the Instant's contrast and print-to-print variation (`Evidence/NativeApp/instant-study-and-toning-059.md`).
With instant-01, instant-03 and instant-04, which the boards already had, they are the 37 prints the study measures; each of the 37 carries a `study` record in `sources.json`.
Licences: CC BY 2.0 (28), CC BY-SA 2.0 (3), Public Domain Mark 1.0 (2), CC0 (1).
Every licence was read from the file's own page and, for Flickr, from Flickr's oEmbed endpoint at download time, and every file is the original size its page offers.
Several show identifiable people; they are for internal look review and would need a separate check before any external use.

How they were found: Wikimedia Commons categories for instant photographs, read through the Commons API and kept where the date is 1972 to 1979, and Flickr's public search pages limited to CC BY, CC BY-SA, CC0, Public Domain Mark, United States Government and no-known-restrictions files.
A print is in the set only where its owner dates it to the 1970s and either states the camera or shows the square picture of integral film.

Left out, and why:

- instant-02, the 1978 office interior the first board paired with a developed scene: the photographer's own copy of it on Flickr (https://www.flickr.com/photos/alan-light/49333638348/) now reads both "Polaroid SX-70 photo" and "Scanned from the original 35MM slide", so it may be a slide copy of the print or not an instant print at all.
- Two prints of the 1978 evening that are published only as enlarged, retouched files (2048 px, with detail the print never held), and one print of the same photographer's that is signed across the picture.
- Prints with an oblong picture, which are peel-apart film or another maker's integral film: among them the National Museum of the U.S. Navy's 1970s series of its park, and several family prints.
- Prints whose pages do not say they are instant prints, modern prints on expired or new film, and pictures saved from an image-sharing app.
