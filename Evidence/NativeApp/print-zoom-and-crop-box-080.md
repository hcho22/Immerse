# Print Zoom and the crop box (080)

On 2026-10-29 the captain asked, from his iPhone: "All cameras - Ability to zoom in on photos after developement and darkroom" and "Darkroom - keep crop function same as native iOS camera app".
He chose the same day:

- "1A": zoom works as in the iPhone's Photos app in the full-screen photo view, on every photo Camera, the Instant print shown at the camera right after an exposure included: pinch or double-tap to zoom in, drag to look around, and swipe to the previous or next print only when zoomed all the way out. Super 8 and 16mm Developed Movies and the Film screen's small prints get no zoom.
- "2A": the Darkroom's Adjust tool crops as the Photos app crops: corner and edge handles on a crop box, pinch and drag the print inside the box, the box re-centers after release, and a rule-of-thirds grid shows while dragging. The shapes are Original, Freeform, Square, 4:3 and 3:2, each sideways or upright. A straighten dial replaces the straightening slider over the same -45° to +45°. Rotate left and Rotate right stay; no flip and no perspective correction. The Instant keeps quarter turns only and no crop, and prints already cropped keep printing exactly as saved.
- "3A": in the Darkroom, pinch or double-tap zooms in and dragging looks around, on every tool except Adjust, where pinching is part of cropping. It is for looking only and never changes the print, and the view returns to the whole print when the tool changes, on Save and on Cancel. He accepted that the Darkroom draws a sharper print while zoomed, which may lag slightly on the oldest supported iPhone after a slider move.
- "8A": on the 6×6 the crop box stays square, as decided on 2026-10-13: the handles resize it as a square and the shapes are hidden, while pinching, dragging and the straighten dial work as on the Disposable.
- "9A": screenshots on the smallest and largest supported iPhone go to him for approval before this ships.

The 2026-10-29 PRD amendment (`2026-09-29-film-camera-experience-v1/2026-10-29-film-camera-experience-v1-prd-phone-feedback.md`) records them as FR-36 (Print Zoom in the photo view, decision 1), FR-37 (Adjust's crop box and straighten dial, decisions 2 and 8) and FR-38 (Print Zoom in the Darkroom, decision 3); FR-36 narrows FR-22's swipe to the whole print and replaces its "no zoom", and FR-37 replaces FR-31's Crop switch, sliders and straightening slider.
"Print Zoom" names looking closer at a developed print; it is not a Zoom Step, the capture lens choice.
Manual steps are Z01 to Z08 in [manual-validation.md](manual-validation.md#print-zoom-and-the-crop-box-on-a-real-iphone).

## What changed

- `App/Immerse/Sources/PrintZoom.swift`, new: `PrintZoom` is a scroll view, as in the Photos app, so its zoom, edge resistance and settling are the system's. Zoom 1 fits the whole print; a double-tap zooms about the tapped point as far as the print fills the view and at least twice, and again shows it whole. Only at the whole print does a sideways swipe of 48 points or more move to the previous or next print; zoomed in, a drag pans. A pinch cancels a swipe begun by its first finger. A new print or `whole` value shows the whole print; a turn of the iPhone keeps the zoom and the point in the middle of the view. An Instant print's margin, hairline edge and shadow are drawn on the zooming view, so they zoom with the card. The furthest zoom puts a pixel of the print on two of the screen's, and is never less than three times the whole. `ZoomedPicture` shows the screen-sized decode first and, once a settled zoom needs more pixels, decodes the print at its own size off the main actor; the sharper picture is let go at the whole print.
- `App/Immerse/Sources/PhotoView.swift`: the photo view's print is `PrintZoom` with the swipe that `DragGesture` made before, given no swipe at the camera's Instant reveal. VoiceOver reads "Zoomed in" as its value while zoomed, and its Previous photo and Next photo actions are unchanged, so they move even while zoomed and the next print shows whole. The Darkroom shows the rendered print through `PrintZoom` on every tool but Adjust, with the tool as its `whole` value; after a slider move the new render replaces the picture in place and is decoded sharp again if still zoomed. An Instant in Adjust shows its print through the same view with zoom off, in the same place. The thumbnail-only `RevealedPhoto` lost its full-screen parameters, and `PrintCardEdge` its large variant, which `PrintZoom` now draws.
- `App/Immerse/Sources/CropEditor.swift`, new: Adjust's crop box over the print without its crop, straightening, vignette or quarter turns (`UncroppedPrint`, which replaces `UnstraightenedPrint`), made once for the recipe's other edits. The box shows exactly what the renderer prints: the renderer crops the turned print and then straightens the crop, keeping the largest centered rectangle of its shape, so the box is that rectangle and the print under it turns about the box's center by the angle and grows with it. Handles (corners 44 points square, edges 44 points deep) resize the box over a still print, freely on Freeform and along the box's diagonal or about its middle on a shape; the farthest part of a drag that keeps the crop on the print and no smaller than a tenth of its shorter side is found by halving. A pinch or drag on the print moves and scales it under a still box, resisting past the print's edge and the largest and smallest crop at three tenths of the finger's travel and settling back. When anything is let go the crop goes to the recipe and the box springs to the middle of the print area, as large as fits inside a 24-point margin. A rule-of-thirds grid shows while anything moves, the dial included. For VoiceOver the box reads how much of the width and height it keeps, and its four edges are adjustable elements that read their place in percent and move a hundredth of the print per swipe. `StraightenDial` is a ruler of degrees under a fixed mark, 5 points a degree, -45° to +45° in tenths, with a selection tap at 0.0°; VoiceOver moves it a whole degree a swipe.
- Adjust's band holds, under its heading and turn buttons, on the Disposable the five shapes and one sideways-or-upright button, which Freeform and Square disable, and then the dial; the 6×6 shows the dial alone. The shapes come first; at large sizes their row scrolls sideways and keeps the chosen shape whole in view. The shapes' text, the dial's angle and the round buttons' symbols stop growing at the largest ordinary text size, as the tool row's icons do, with the Large Content Viewer, so the shapes and the whole dial open in view at every text size and the symbols stay inside their circles. A print opens on the shape its saved crop has (Freeform without one), choosing a shape gives the crop that shape about the same area and center, made to fit the print, and a quarter turn turns a shape's sides with the crop.
- The controls band is 250 points tall where it was 330: Adjust's five rows of sliders set it, and the tallest tool now is Filtration at 249 points with its three sliders on an iPhone SE at the default text size (Adjust needs 243). The print area is therefore 80 points taller on every iPhone, 221 points on an iPhone SE where it was 141, and still one size whatever the tool.
- `Packages/RenderCore/Sources/RenderCore/DarkroomCrop.swift`, new: `CropShape` and `CropGeometry`, the crop box's arithmetic in the turned print's pixels: a rectangle to a recipe `Crop` that the renderer's validation accepts as it is (fractions never past an edge in floating point, equal on the 6×6, nil for the whole print), what straightening leaves of a crop and back, the smallest crop, a shape's crop and the shape a saved crop has.
- Nothing in the renderer, the recipe or what it stores changed: a crop is still `x`, `y`, `width` and `height`, and the chosen shape is not stored but read back from the crop, so every recipe saved by the build before decodes and prints byte for byte as it did, and a revert reads every recipe saved by this build.
- Tests: `Packages/RenderCore/Tests/RenderCoreTests/DarkroomCropTests.swift` (9 tests); `App/Immerse/Tests/JournalIntegrationTests.swift` holds `UncroppedPrint` to the edits it is made for; `Probes/PopulatedJournalHarness/Tests/PrintZoomWorkflowTests.swift` (10 tests, below); `PopulatedWorkflowTests` and `RetainedWorkflowTests` look for the crop box and the dial where they looked for the Crop switch and the sliders, measure the Adjust region by its box, and hold the 250-point band. The harness passes `TEST_RUNNER_CONTENT_SIZE` on as the text size for screenshots (its README).

## Fixes from the review of 2026-10-29

- On Freeform a dragged corner follows one rule, the captain's of 2026-10-30: on every event it goes to the finger where the crop allows it there, and otherwise to the corner position the crop allows that is nearest the finger, worked out from where the gesture began and nothing else (`CropEditor.handled`).
So a corner whose one edge is held, at the print's edge or at the smallest crop, even held both ways, still moves its other edge with the finger, as in the Photos app, where before the whole drag stopped as soon as either edge did, and on a straightened print, where an edge's travel moves the crop along both sides of the print, the corner slides along a limit with the finger.
The positions the crop allows are not a polygon to project the finger onto: on a straightened print the crop's longer side grows with the square of the box's longer side over its shorter one, so its limits curve (traced at +3°, one limit's slope runs from 0.002 to 0.023 over 60 points).
So the nearest position is looked for along 64 straight lines from where the corner began, the four along the axes exactly so, the point nearest the finger on the part of each the crop allows, kept only where the crop allows that point, and refined between the lines either side of the nearest.
Where the crop touches a print edge, the positions it allows can lie, seen from where the corner began, in a wedge narrower than the lines' spacing; so wherever two neighbouring lines disagree on whether the crop allows their point nearest the finger, the edge between them is found by halving, and the nearest point of the line just inside it is a candidate too.
Where an edge of the box is held both ways, under the smallest box or at the smallest crop against a print edge, the positions the crop allows are a line or a wedge whose sides run along the box's edges or the print's as the box shows them, turned by the straightening angle either way; the lines along those are candidates too.
A candidate replaces the nearest only when strictly nearer, and the first line wins an exact tie, so the same finger always gives the same corner.
A shape, the 6×6 and an edge go as far along the drag as the crop allows, found by halving, as before.
No handle passes the box's other side: a box turned inside out, with its width or height below nothing, is never allowed, where before the size check read `CGRect`'s width and height, which are never negative, and let a corner dragged across a narrow box turn it onto the other side.
A shape and the 6×6's square still move both edges of a corner together, along the shape.
- A tight crop is sharp in Adjust, as it was before this change: where the box at rest shows the print at more pixels than its screen-sized picture has, the crop box draws from the print's own pixels through the same `ZoomedPicture` Print Zoom uses, decoded only then and let go at the whole print and as Adjust closes.
On an iPhone SE that is any 3:2 crop keeping less than about 39% of the print's width.
